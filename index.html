The AI Dossier

A dossier ships with every homework: a fixed-form record of how you used AI. It serves two purposes — fast grading, and accountability. You report the models, your prompts, the rough cost, and one non-trivial mistake you caught.

How it is graded

Two passes, and the first is mechanical:

  1. Run ./check_dossier.sh DOSSIER.md (or DOSSIER_LITE.md) in your repo. Red = the dossier is ungraded, full stop — it enforces the form and the length caps.
  2. Green → the grader reads your reasoning: does the named mistake cite real evidence (a probe value, a diagnostic, a failing test) rather than vibes?

You run the same checker the grader runs — check_dossier.sh ships in every starter repo.

The dossier is the capped reflection. The unabridged record behind it is your AI transcripts, submitted with the same homework — the dossier says what you learned, the transcripts show the work.

Templates

Download the form your homework calls for, fill it in, and commit it at the root of your homework repo. Both are also seeded in every starter repo — these copies are the authoritative version if the two ever disagree.

  • DOSSIER_LITE.md — thin homeworks (HW1, HW3, HW4). Three fields, capped at 10 content lines total.
  • DOSSIER.md — heavy homeworks (HW2, HW5, HW6). Five fields; the mistake field caps at 5 content lines and what I’d pin next time at 2.
  • check_dossier.sh — the checker itself, if you need to re-download it.
A content line is a non-empty line you write inside a field — headings, <!-- hint --> lines, and blank lines don’t count. Two things fail the check more often than anything else: leaving a (your entry) placeholder in place, and retyping a heading by hand. The checker matches headings byte-for-byte, em dash and arrow included, so edit around them rather than retyping them.
