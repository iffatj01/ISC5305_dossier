Quiz score calculator

Questions are not worth the same. A multi-step trace is worth three times a definition, because it is three times the work and three times the evidence that you understand the material. Tick every question you answered correctly — the running total updates as you go.

L1 — Recall / concept 1 point each
L2 — Short code 2 points each
L3 — Multi-step trace 3 points each

How the arithmetic works

A quiz has 22 questions worth 33 points:

Class What it is Count Points each Subtotal
L1 recall / concept 14 1 14
L2 short code, one concept 5 2 10
L3 multi-step trace 3 3 9
22 33

Full credit is 30 points, not 33. Three points are forgiven — that is the safety allowance, and it exists so that a question which turns out to be unclear, ambiguous, or simply wrong cannot cost you anything. You can spend it on one hard question or three easy ones, and either way you still score 100%.

Below 30 points the grade is proportional: grade = points ÷ 30. Missing four easy questions gives 29/30 ≈ 97%; missing all three traces gives 24/30 = 80%.

The allowance is deliberately not counted in questions. If it were, the cheapest strategy would be to skip the two hardest questions and take them for free — so the allowance is measured in points, and skipping a 3-point trace costs the whole allowance rather than one of two free passes.

What this does not tell you

This calculator reports one quiz. It does not know your homework grades, and it does not apply any curve. If a quiz turns out harder than intended, grades are adjusted upward afterwards — never downward — so the number here is a floor, not a verdict.
